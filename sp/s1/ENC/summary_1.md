# Response
In the passage, James E. Porter explains the concept of a theory within the domain of technical writing, eventually providing a theory to be utilized within the process of technical authorship.
He presents an array of definitions for the term 'Theory', choosing the idea that a theory is "a conceptual framework [..] that provides [..] a way of looking at the world,"(pg 128).
Porter demonstrates that a theory can be used as a heuristic to analyze and better understand technical communication.
Porter explains that a theory determines the manner in which a technical communicator views both their subject matter and their eventual output.
Whether they are attempting to create a website documenting a particular business practice or developing a content management system for an entire organization, a theory should guide the author throughout the entire process.

After establishing the conceptual framework _theory-as-a-heuristic_ Porter presents a rhetorical heuristic designed to enhance an author's understanding of the system they are working within.
Essentially, authors should turn their attention to the overall process "that users are engaged in" (pg 140) and design their final product to fit within needs of the users.
A proficient piece of technical documentation adapts to it's audience, dynamically shifting mediums where necessary, and is written by an author that has a deep understanding of their users.
To ensure such an output Porter demonstrates throughout the passage how rhetorical theories can be utilized to develop such an intricate perception of both the technical needs of a system and audience that will use it.
 
Porter spent a great deal of effort to properly define the concepts presented in the passage. 
As a result of this detail the conclusions reached within are as focused as the practical advice that Porter espouses.
Of which I do agree with the general premise of the passage.
