Professional Information Product
The Challenge

Professional writers are often asked to communicate information they already know to people who do not share their knowledge, experience, or priorities. Your task is to transform something you already know into a professional document that is useful to a specific audience.

You will select a topic you know well, identify a specific audience, determine what that audience needs, select an appropriate genre, and create a polished professional document.

Your goal is not simply to tell your audience what you know. Your goal is to determine what your audience needs to know and create a document that helps them use that information.
Your Topic

Select something you already know well. Your topic might relate to:

-your major or academic field
-career or profession
-hobby or interest
-technology
-campus organization or activity
-skill
-an experience
-a community
-a process
-a resource
-subject you have significant experience with

You do not need to conduct extensive research. The focus of this project is on communicating existing knowledge effectively.
Your Audience

Identify a specific audience who could benefit from your information.

Avoid audiences that are too broad, such as "everyone," "students," or "the public."

Instead, consider audiences such as:

-first-year students who are considering a major
-students preparing for their first internship
-people who are beginners in a particular hobby
-parents unfamiliar with a particular activity
-students considering changing majors
-new members of an organization
-people entering a particular profession
Your Genre

Select a genre that makes sense for your audience and purpose.

Possible genres include:

-flyer
-brochure
-newsletter
-FAQ
-guide
-infographic
-fact sheet
-webpage mockup
-orientation document
-promotional document
-resource guide
-"How To" guide
-career guide
-another professional genre pre-approved by your instructor
Your Document Must

Your final document should:

-establish a clear purpose
-address a specific audience
-use an appropriate professional genre
-provide information relevant to that audience
-organize information logically
-use an appropriate tone
-demonstrate intentional visual/design choices
-be easy to scan and navigate
-provide an appropriate next step or call to action
-demonstrate professional editing and formatting
-incorporate useful feedback from your prototype review
Rhetorical Rationale

Professional writers must be able to explain and justify the choices they make. After completing your Professional Information Product, write a 750\u20131,000 word rhetorical rationale explaining the decisions behind your document.

Address the following:

Audience

Who is your intended audience? What does your audience know, need, value, or expect?

Purpose

What did you want your audience to understand, believe, or do?

Genre

Why did you select this genre? What conventions did you follow?

Content

How did you decide what information to include, emphasize, or exclude?

Organization

Why did you organize the information in this particular way?

Design

How do your visual choices support your audience and purpose?

Tone and Language

Why did you make particular choices about tone, vocabulary, terminology, or level of formality?

Support From Assigned Class Readings

Your rationale should use at least two assigned readings from module one to support your decision making.
