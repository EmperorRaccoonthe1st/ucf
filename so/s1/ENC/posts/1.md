# Post

Within the field of software development 'Software Requirements Specification (SRS)' and 'Quality Assurance Documentation (QA)' are utilized often within businesses to communicate between teams and their project goals.

- 'Software Requirements Specification': Readers interact with these documents when architecting their overall project and designing the time frames from which to operate within. It details the exact features and behaviors that the resulting piece of software must contain. Business documents like Business Requirements Document & Product Requirements Document provide the high level intent behind the initial development of the software, while System Design Documents & High Level Design further refine the specifications presented into actual technical architecture. SRS's serve as the central connector between the lofty business goals and the low level design choices required to carry them out.

- 'Quality Assurance Documentation (QA)': Readers interact with these while designing tests to perform on applications in development. QA doc's are essential as they determine what aspects of the applications should be tested and to what extent they must comply. A central source of truth leads a software development team, allowing them to work efficiently together to ensure no undesired functionality. QA could not be completed without the use of bug report document, which captures errors within the application. The goal of Quality Assurance and documentation surrounding it is to identify issues, capture them within bug reports, and update the application to repair them.

I am most drawn to SRS's because designing the scope of an application and adapting it's functionality over time is very appealing to me. Having the ability to sculpt what the final product will eventually be is interesting and part of the reason that I wanted to go into this field to begin with.

For how these interact with other documents, I think they’d both be a great addition to documents with similar themes.
