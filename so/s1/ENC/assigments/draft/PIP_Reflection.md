# Prompt
Reflective Writing: From Professional Information Product to Transfer
Purpose

The Professional Information Product asked you to take information you already know and transform it into a document designed for a specific professional audience and purpose. In doing so, you made decisions about audience, purpose, genre, organization, tone, design, and the kinds of information your readers would need.

This reflection asks you to look beyond the finished product and consider what you learned from the process. The goal is not simply to describe what you did, but to identify writing knowledge and strategies that you can transfer to other situations—including your other UCF courses, future academic writing, internships, jobs, and other professional environments.
Your Task

Write a reflection that explains what you learned about writing for professional environments through the Professional Information Product project and how you can apply that knowledge beyond this assignment.

Your reflection should address the following questions:

## 1. What did you learn about writing for a professional audience?

Identify one or two important things you learned about professional writing through this project.

Consider questions such as:

    How did you determine what your audience needed to know?
    How did your audience influence the information you included or left out?
    How did you adjust your tone or level of formality?
    What did you learn about organizing information for readers?
    How did visual design, headings, formatting, or other features affect your document?
    How did the genre of your document influence the way you communicated?
    What did you discover about the difference between writing something you know and writing something that is useful to someone else?

Use specific examples from your project to explain your answer.

## 2. What writing decisions did you make, and why?

Choose one significant decision you made while creating your Professional Information Product.

Explain:

    What decision did you make?
    What alternatives did you consider?
    What did you want the decision to accomplish for your audience?
    How did feedback, revision, or your own evaluation influence the decision?

Think of this section as an opportunity to explain the rhetorical reasoning behind your choices rather than simply describing the steps you took.

## 3. Where else can you use this knowledge?

Now consider how the knowledge or strategies you developed through this project could transfer to another writing situation.

Identify at least one specific situation outside this course where you could apply what you learned.

This might include:

    a writing assignment in another UCF course
    a research paper or project
    a group project
    an internship or campus organization
    an application, résumé, or cover letter
    an email to a professor, supervisor, or employer
    a presentation
    a future workplace writing situation
    a document you may need to create in your future career

Describe the situation and explain how you would approach it differently because of what you learned in this project.

## 4. What will you do differently as a writer?

Identify one specific writing habit or strategy you want to carry forward.

Complete the following idea in your own words:

When I encounter a new writing situation in the future, I will...

Explain why you have chosen this strategy and how you expect it to help you become a more effective writer.
Reflection Guidelines

Your reflection should:

    Be approximately 750 words
    Refer to specific decisions, examples, or experiences from your Professional Information Product
    Focus on what you learned and how you can use it, rather than simply summarizing the project
    Make connections to at least one writing situation outside this course
    Use a reflective but professional tone
    Be organized so that your ideas are easy for a reader to follow

A Useful Way to Think About Transfer

Transfer means taking knowledge or strategies you learned in one situation and recognizing when and how they can be useful in a new situation.

For example:

    What I did in this project: I identified what information my audience would actually need rather than including everything I knew about the topic.

    What I learned: Effective writing requires making decisions based on the audience's needs rather than the writer's knowledge.

    Where I can use it: In a research paper for another course, I can think about what background information my particular readers need instead of assuming they have the same knowledge I do.

Your reflection should make a similar connection between what you did → what you learned → where you can use it next.

# Response

Throughout the process of authoring my _Professional Information Project_ I have gained critical experience within the domain of technical writing.
This experience will be utilized not only to enhance my future technical writing but throughout my career within the Information Technology space.
Authoring my technical guide required honing my ability to analyze intricate systems and select the most pertinent information for synthesis into a final product.
Throughout the process of building the document proper editing was essential to ensuring that the guide would be accessible by its target audience.
With each instruction presented in a tone that would allow the audience to follow them with ease.

The editing process necessary to build a proper document for the _Professional Information Project_ was a highly instructional exercise in crafting a professional, educational tone.
Through authoring my guide I acquired the ability to cater the tone of my writing for a professional audience.
The tone of a document is the vital undercurrent on which the information presented within resides.
The content of a piece is shaped by the tone in which it is presented in.
I have learned that in a professional context the tone must remain formal while utilizing an inflection that captures the reader's attention.
Each piece of information should be defined in clear terms which lack room for interpretation. 
Significant details should be featured primarily both within the structure of an individual sentence and within the overall visual format of the document.

A major decision taken in the design of the document was the choice to utilize charts and tables as the primary format to convey the myriad instructions required to operate _Vim_.
Within the guide it was necessary, once the fundamentals were established, to introduce the audience to the most common commands used within the different modes of _Vim_. 
Such an introduction would result in around ten new pieces of distinct information being covered simultaneously.
If one were to attempt to approach this dynamic through a traditional textual means the audience would be completely overwhelmed and unable to properly utilize the guide.
Thus I decided on a different, visual, method to convey this deluge of information in a manner much more approachable to the audience.
By opting for a series of charts which isolated each command to a single line, the audience is able to direct their focus on a command in an incremental manner.
They can process an individual command at their own pace, row by row.
With each row presenting how to access a command, its behavior, and how it can be utilized within an code editing context.
In this way the audience is presented with one concept in orderly fashion, not overloaded by an unassailable wall of text.

The skills and techniques I gained from working on the _Professional Information Project_ are highly versatile.
I will often in my career be required to convey technical information, either about a project, specific technology, or concept utilized within my organization.
Each of these applications will require me to meticulously compose my work with the proper formal tone and scope that is expected within a professional organization.
The immediate example of an impactful scenario in which I will utilize the skills acquired from this project is within my upcoming _Senior Design Project_.
This is a component of a Computer Science degree at UCF where students are required to work with a large group of their peers to build a technical, professional-grade project.
Students typically are given an entire semester to finish this project, and it is evaluated with as if it were developed by a group of professionals within the industry.
This undertaking will require numerous documents like guides, reports, or presentations which will be shaped by the skills developed within my _Professional Information Project_.
I will have to consider the preexisting knowledge and expectations of my audience as I decide on which aspects of my topic I should include, emphasize, and omit.
Ensuring that the tone in which it is delivered enhances their understanding of the topic, through clear decisive language.

The most salient skill I gained, the one which I will utilize when authoring every subsequent piece, is the ability to analyze my intended audience and design the document to serve their comprehension.
Every deliverable is written for a targeted audience, or in the very least for the author in the future.
Therefore purposefully designing the piece for an audience through taking into account their specific necessities and designing it in a manner to suite them, will ensure that it properly suites its intended purpose.
No other strategy cultivated through the _Professional Information Project_ has such an impact on the results of a document than taking into account its audience.
