# Prompt

This is a multi-step assignment. Please allow yourself enough time to complete effectively. 
Step 1: Find an entry-level  job posting in your future field.
Recommended sources for job advertisements
1. Indeed

Best all-purpose starting point

Search a wide range of industries and job levels, including entry-level positions, internships, and full-time roles. Useful for comparing how different employers describe similar positions.

indeed.com

 2. LinkedIn

Best for professional and corporate roles

Particularly useful for marketing, communications, human resources, business, technology, public relations, and management positions. Students can also explore how employers describe desired professional skills.

linkedin.com/jobs

3. Handshake- this is free to UCF students. If you don't already have an account, this is the perfect time to create one. 

Best for college students and recent graduates

A strong place to look for internships, early-career positions, and employers who recruit college students. UCF students should check their university career-services resources for access.

joinhandshake.com

4. USAJOBS

Best for government and public service careers

Ideal for students interested in public administration, environmental science, communications, policy, research, and other federal careers. Federal announcements can be especially useful for examining formal qualifications and detailed application requirements.
Find your future in federal service
usajobs.gov

5. O*NET OnLine

Best for exploring unfamiliar career paths

O*NET helps students identify occupational titles, typical responsibilities, skills, and related professional associations. It is not primarily a job board, so I would use it to identify search terms and then locate actual job advertisements elsewhere. 

onetonline.org

6. Idealist

Best for nonprofit and social-impact careers

Worth exploring for students interested in nonprofit management, advocacy, community engagement, social services, and mission-driven communication.

idealist.org

# Response

Within the field of software development, the entry-level posting titled "Computer Science, Software Engineers, Programmers" on Indeed represents an opportunity at Thermoflow, a Jacksonville, Florida company founded by a former MIT faculty member that builds power plant design software for leading energy organizations across eighty countries. In this position, engineers assist in building the next generation of Thermoflow's software suite by transitioning legacy PC-based tools into a web-hosted environment. To carry out this migration, the organization seeks recent Computer Science graduates who demonstrate an outstanding academic record, practical knowledge of web-based application development, and an understanding of internet and intranet architecture alongside associated security issues. Essential keywords derived directly from the posting include _Computer Science_, _software development_, _web-based applications_, _web-hosted environment_, _internet/intranet architecture_, and _security issues_.

Currently, I meet several foundational qualifications for this role through my Computer Science coursework at UCF, where I maintain strong academic standing while developing proficiency in object-oriented programming, system design, and technical documentation. My experience authoring applications across Java, Python, and C within Linux environments aligns closely with Thermoflow's emphasis on disciplined software engineering and a genuine passion for code. However, before applying, I could utilize more hands-on experience with modern production web application frameworks and distributed enterprise network security. Because Thermoflow is actively migrating complex desktop engineering systems into web-hosted platforms, strengthening my practical expertise in scalable web deployment, client-server security protocols, and network architecture will ensure that I am thoroughly prepared to contribute effectively within their technical team.
