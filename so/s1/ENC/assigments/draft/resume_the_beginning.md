In the eighth chapter of _Writing for the Workplace_ Janet Mizrahi deconstructs the written job application process and illustrates how to properly advocate for oneself with a prospective employer.
They begin by depicting the process and content of a cover letter, establishing the document as a foundation to a proper job application.
A methodology for building cover letter is defined, analyzing each section across both purpose and format.

I have currently never used a cover letter when applying for a position.
This is an area of the application process I am completely lacking in due to my nonexistent experience. 
The tutorial which Mizrahi provided will serve as an excellent guide for when I build my first letters for my preliminary positions within industry.

The author continues their analysis to the central component of all job applications, the resume.
The analysis approaches the document across every metric, covering format, design, and language.
In particular the section concerning the relevancy of experience listed on the resume will assist in enhancing my resume most directly.

Mizrahi defines clear boundaries about what experience should be included within a chronological resume, the format I currently utilize.
The listed experience must be focused to the field of the position one is applying to, contain distinct action oriented descriptions, and should be particularly recent.
My current resume, which has not received an update for many months, still displays experience from high school positions.
After reviewing the instructions provided relayed within this chapter, I now understand the reason why these listing must be edited and what content to replace them with to ensure a professional, representative resume.
