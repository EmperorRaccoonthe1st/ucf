# 11/08/26

## Best Practices
- **Focusing on Clean Position Descriptions**: An integral component of building an appealing, professional essay is authoring apt descriptions of an applicants past positions. This phase in the resume building process can require a decent investment in time, one which I have payed completely over the years. Is is through this iterative process of refining the descriptions featured in the _experiences_ section that I have learned the most capable tone and diction to describe my past positions with.

## Worst Practices
- **Unclear Formatting**: In the past I have not built a proper format for my resumes. I drafted them with a confusing concoction of bulleted points and colons, with bolded & underlined text littered throughout the document. These structures were attempts at organizing the information in a productive manner, but instead only served to increase the visual clutter of my resume and detract from my nascent professional persona. I have learned now how to properly design and format a polished resume to modern standards.

- **Unfocused Career History**: For a significant period of time the _experience_ section of my resume contained juvenile past positions like my first job as a math tutor. While essential for obtaining my literal first job in high school, they were not suited for any other positions I would applied to in the future. When revising my resume for latter applications in the following years I lacked the understanding that to prompt me to edit them out of the document. It was only after receiving review from my family members and coworkers that I finally removed the antiquated entries in the section.

# 8/29/26

## Best Practices
- *Vocabulary*: In almost every serious sentence I put down I will review for grammar, punctuation, & specifically for vocabulary. I ensure that every verb, noun, or adverb is specifically chosen to reflect the context of my current piece and always aims for a high level of complexity/detail. It is very rare for me to utilize a "simple" word or phrase when I am writing, as I really do spend a considerable amount of time choosing the proper, advanced, term for the situation.

## Worst Practices
- *Run-On Sentences*: Run-On sentences are when an author places too many clauses into a single sentence, through commas or repetitive uses of conjunctions, instead of utilizing multiple related sentences. I often find myself writing Run-On sentences when I want to express a complex though. I have to slow down, evaluate my last sentence, and split it into multiple complete sentences.
