## Detailed Explanations & Rationales

### Question 1
> **For the reaction F --> G, $\Delta G = -350$. Which of the following is true?**
- **Correct Answer:** **The reaction is exergonic, it gives off energy.**
- **Rationale:** 
  - A negative change in Gibbs free energy ($\Delta G < 0$) defines an **exergonic** reaction.
  - Exergonic reactions occur spontaneously and release (give off) net free energy to their surroundings. 
  - An endergonic reaction has a positive $\Delta G$ ($\Delta G > 0$) and requires energy input.

---

### Question 2
> **For the reaction E --> F, $\Delta G = -220$. Which of the following reactions could be coupled with this reaction?**
- **Correct Answer:** **S --> T, $\Delta G = 150$**
- **Rationale:** 
  - In energetic reaction coupling, an exergonic reaction drives an endergonic reaction as long as the net change in free energy is negative:
    $$\Delta G_{\text{net}} = \Delta G_1 + \Delta G_2 < 0$$
  - Evaluating each option:
    - **$S \rightarrow T$ ($\Delta G = +150$):** $\Delta G_{\text{net}} = -220 + 150 = -70 < 0$ (**Favorable / Spontaneous**)
    - $D \rightarrow H$ ($\Delta G = -250$): Already exergonic; does not require driving by another reaction.
    - $M \rightarrow L$ ($\Delta G = +225$): $\Delta G_{\text{net}} = -220 + 225 = +5 > 0$ (Requires more energy than is released).
    - $O \rightarrow P$ ($\Delta G = +250$): $\Delta G_{\text{net}} = -220 + 250 = +30 > 0$ (Insufficient energy).

### Question 5
> **Per molecule of glucose, how much ATP is produced during pyruvate oxidation?**
- **Correct Answer:** **0**
- **Rationale:** 
  - Pyruvate oxidation links glycolysis to the citric acid cycle. Per glucose molecule (2 pyruvates):
    $$\text{2 Pyruvate} + 2\text{ NAD}^+ + 2\text{ CoA} \rightarrow 2\text{ Acetyl-CoA} + 2\text{ NADH} + 2\text{ CO}_2$$
  - No ATP or GTP is formed by substrate-level phosphorylation during this step.

### Question 6
> **Which of the following correctly pairs a product of the CAC with what happens to it? Hint: Remember that each pyruvate goes through the CAC, you need the total for both pyruvate molecules.**
- **Correct Answer:** **2 FADH2: goes to Complex II of the ETC**
- **Rationale:** 
  - Verified from Slide 5 & 6 board photos [`deck_answers/PXL_20261012_151543903.RAW-01.jpg`](file:///home/owen/ucf/so/s1/BSC/block3/active_learning/deck_answers/PXL_20261012_151543903.RAW-01.jpg) & [`deck_answers/PXL_20261012_152016848.RAW-01.jpg`](file:///home/owen/ucf/so/s1/BSC/block3/active_learning/deck_answers/PXL_20261012_152016848.RAW-01.jpg):
    - For 2 turns of the CAC (from 1 glucose): $6\text{ NADH}$, $2\text{ FADH}_2$, $2\text{ ATP}$, and $4\text{ CO}_2$ are generated.
    - $\text{FADH}_2$ donates electrons specifically to **Complex II** (succinate dehydrogenase).
    - $\text{NADH}$ donates electrons to **Complex I** (*not* Complex III).
    - $\text{CO}_2$ is exhaled as a waste product.
    - Proton pumping across the inner mitochondrial membrane is powered by the redox reactions of the ETC, not ATP.

### Question 7
> **Beginning with FADH2 track the flow of electrons through the ETC.**
- **Correct Answer:** **Complex II, Ubiquinone, Complex III, Ctyochrome C, Complex IV, oxygen**
- **Rationale:** 
  - As highlighted on Slide 6 (*"Follow the GREEN $\rightarrow$ PURPLE path"*):
    1. $\text{FADH}_2$ is oxidized at **Complex II**.
    2. Electrons transfer to the lipid-soluble carrier **Ubiquinone (Q)**.
    3. Ubiquinone shuttles them to **Complex III**.
    4. Complex III transfers them to the peripheral protein **Cytochrome c**.
    5. Cytochrome c transfers them to **Complex IV**.
    6. Complex IV transfers them to molecular **Oxygen** ($\text{O}_2$), which is reduced to water.

### Question 8
> **What is fixed during the Fixation phase of the Calvin Cycle?**
- **Correct Answer:** **carbon dioxide**
- **Rationale:** 
  - In Phase 1 of the Calvin Cycle (Carbon Fixation), atmospheric inorganic $\text{CO}_2$ is catalyzed by the enzyme **Rubisco** to combine with the 5-carbon sugar ribulose 1,5-bisphosphate (RuBP), fixing inorganic carbon into an organic molecule.

### Question 10
> **Based on the Active Learning Activity, which part of photosyntheis is impacted by herbicide H-1?**
- **Correct Answer:** **Photosystem I**
- **Rationale:** 
  - Look at the Herbicide table on Slide 9 ([`deck_answers/PXL_20261012_153822713.RAW-01.jpg`](file:///home/owen/ucf/so/s1/BSC/block3/active_learning/deck_answers/PXL_20261012_153822713.RAW-01.jpg)):
    - **H-1 Profile:**
      - Chlorophyll Absorption: *Normal*
      - Oxygen Production: *Normal* (PS II splits water normally)
      - NADPH Production: **Decreased** (PS I cannot pass electrons to $\text{NADP}^+$)
      - ATP Synthase Activity: *Normal* (cytochrome complex proton pumping is intact)
      - G3P Production: **Decreased** (Calvin cycle lacks NADPH)
    - Therefore, **H-1 inhibits Photosystem I**.
