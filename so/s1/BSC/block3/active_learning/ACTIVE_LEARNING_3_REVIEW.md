### Slide 2: Reaction Coupling (Thermodynamics & $\Delta G$)

#### Part 1: Reaction $A \rightarrow B$ ($\Delta G = -200$)
1. **Is $A \rightarrow B$ endergonic or exergonic?**
   - **Exergonic** ($\Delta G < 0$, spontaneous process).
2. **Does it require energy or give off energy?**
   - **Gives off energy** (releases free energy to the surroundings).
3. **Which reaction could be coupled with $A \rightarrow B$?**
   - Candidate reactions:
     - $C \rightarrow D$, $\Delta G = -350$ (Exergonic; does not require driving)
     - $E \rightarrow F$, $\Delta G = +375$ (Net $\Delta G = -200 + 375 = +175 > 0$; insufficient energy)
     - $G \rightarrow H$, $\Delta G = +250$ (Net $\Delta G = -200 + 250 = +50 > 0$; insufficient energy)
     - **$I \rightarrow J$, $\Delta G = +175$** (Net $\Delta G = -200 + 175 = -25 < 0$; **Spontaneous**)
   - **Answer**: **$I \rightarrow J$ ($\Delta G = 175$)**
   - *Key Principle*: Energetic coupling requires $\Delta G_{\text{net}} = \Delta G_1 + \Delta G_2 < 0$. The energy released by the exergonic reaction ($-200$) must exceed the energy consumed by the endergonic reaction ($+175$).

#### Part 2: Reaction $X \rightarrow Y$ ($\Delta G = +75$)
1. **Is $X \rightarrow Y$ endergonic or exergonic?**
   - **Endergonic** ($\Delta G > 0$, non-spontaneous process).
2. **Does it require energy or give off energy?**
   - **Requires energy** (absorbs free energy).
3. **Which reaction could be coupled with $X \rightarrow Y$?**
   - Candidate reactions:
     - **$C \rightarrow D$, $\Delta G = -125$** (Net $\Delta G = +75 + (-125) = -50 < 0$; **Spontaneous**)
     - $E \rightarrow F$, $\Delta G = +225$ (Endergonic; cannot drive another reaction)
     - $G \rightarrow H$, $\Delta G = -50$ (Net $\Delta G = +75 + (-50) = +25 > 0$; insufficient energy)
     - $I \rightarrow J$, $\Delta G = +100$ (Endergonic)
   - **Answer**: **$C \rightarrow D$ ($\Delta G = -125$)**

### Slide 3: Enzyme Inhibition: Competitive or Noncompetitive?

#### Biological Context:
- **Enzyme**: Acetylcholinesterase degrades acetylcholine (neurotransmitter causing muscle stimulation) so muscles can relax.

#### Questions & Visual Analysis:
1. **What kind of inhibitor is Sarin?**
   - **Answer**: **Noncompetitive (Allosteric) inhibitor**
   - *Reasoning based on the slide diagram*: Sarin (the blue sphere) is shown binding to an alternate site on the enzyme (allosteric site) rather than into the V-shaped active site. Upon Sarin binding, the enzyme undergoes a conformational change that alters the geometry of the active site (from a V-notch to a square notch), preventing the substrate (acetylcholine) from binding.
2. **Based on shape, what kind of inhibitor would you expect Mullerian to be?**
   - **Answer**: **Competitive inhibitor**
   - *Reasoning*: Mullerian is depicted as a triangle/wedge shape that directly mimics the substrate's V-shaped binding region. Because its shape is complementary to the enzyme's active site, it will bind directly to the active site, competing with acetylcholine.

### Slide 6: Cellular Respiration – Oxidative Phosphorylation
*(Corresponds to classroom board photo: [`PXL_20261012_152016848.RAW-01.jpg`](file:///home/owen/ucf/so/s1/BSC/block3/active_learning/deck_answers/PXL_20261012_152016848.RAW-01.jpg))*

1. **$H^+$ ions are transported from the mitochondrial `Matrix` into the `Intermembrane space`.**
2. **Is this active transport or passive transport?**
   - **Active transport (against the concentration gradient)**
3. **If active transport, what provides the energy for this?**
   - **Energy stored in bonds/electrons of NADH and FADH2 (electron carriers)** (released exergonically as electrons pass down the redox chain).
4. **Beginning with NADH, track the flow of electrons through the ETC:**
   - **Follow the RED $\rightarrow$ PURPLE path** (Enters at **Complex I** $\rightarrow$ Ubiquinone (Q) $\rightarrow$ **Complex III** $\rightarrow$ Cytochrome c $\rightarrow$ **Complex IV** $\rightarrow$ $O_2$).
5. **Beginning with FADH2, track the flow of electrons through the ETC:**
   - **Follow the GREEN $\rightarrow$ PURPLE path** (Enters at **Complex II** $\rightarrow$ Ubiquinone (Q) $\rightarrow$ **Complex III** $\rightarrow$ Cytochrome c $\rightarrow$ **Complex IV** $\rightarrow$ $O_2$).
6. **What is the final electron acceptor in the ETC?**
   - **Oxygen ($O_2$)** (reduced along with $H^+$ to form $H_2O$).
7. **What powers the production of ATP by ATP Synthase?**
   - **The $H^+$ concentration gradient; $H^+$ moving back into the mitochondrial matrix (High to low)** (Chemiosmosis via proton-motive force through ATP Synthase).

### Slide 7: Cellular Poisons – Match the Symptom to the Poison
*(Corresponds to classroom board photo: [`PXL_20261012_152558892.RAW-01.jpg`](file:///home/owen/ucf/so/s1/BSC/block3/active_learning/deck_answers/PXL_20261012_152558892.RAW-01.jpg))*

| Poison | Mechanism | Matched Symptom | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Shikonin** | Inhibits breakdown of glucose | **B** | Glucose cannot enter glycolysis, resulting in extremely high intracellular glucose levels. Without pyruvate downstream, the CAC and oxidative phosphorylation shut down, causing extremely low ATP production. |
| **Arsenic** | Inhibits oxidation of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA | **D** | Glycolysis functions normally (glucose levels normal), but pyruvate cannot be converted to acetyl-CoA, causing pyruvate buildup. Downstream CAC and oxidative phosphorylation are starved, producing negligible ATP. |
| **Rotenone** | Disrupts Complex I to Ubiquinone | **C** | Complex I is blocked, preventing NADH from donating electrons (NADH levels rise). FADH2 donates electrons downstream at Complex II, leaving FADH2 consumption intact (normal levels). ATP output is reduced because only Complex II-driven electron flow occurs. |
| **DNP** | Makes mitochondrial inner membrane leaky to $H^+$ | **E** | Uncouples electron transport from ATP synthesis: protons leak across the inner membrane directly into the matrix, collapsing the proton gradient (lower $H^+$ in intermembrane space, higher $H^+$ in matrix). ATP production drops drastically. |
| **Oligomycin** | Blocks ATP synthase | **A** | Protons continue being pumped out of the matrix by the ETC into the intermembrane space, but cannot re-enter through ATP synthase. This leads to a massive accumulation of $H^+$ in the intermembrane space and minimal ATP synthesis. |

### Slide 8: Concept Map of Photosynthesis in Chloroplast
*(Corresponds to classroom board photo: [`PXL_20261012_153537276.RAW-01.jpg`](file:///home/owen/ucf/so/s1/BSC/block3/active_learning/deck_answers/PXL_20261012_153537276.RAW-01.jpg))*

```
                 [ H2O ]
                    |
                    v
  [ O2 ] <--- [ PS II ] <==== [absorption of Light]
                 | (contains Chlorophyll)
                 | (donates e-)
                 v
        [ Cytochrome Complex ] <--- pumps [ Protons (H+) ]
                 |                               |
                 | (passes e-)                   v
                 v                        [ ATP Synthase ]
          [ Photosystem I ]                      | (synthesizes)
                 | (contains Chlorophyll)         v
                 | (absorption of Light)       [ ATP ] -------\
                 | (transfers Electrons)                       \
                 v                                              v
             [ NADPH ] --------------------------------> [ Calvin Cycle ] <--- [ CO2 ]
                                                                |
                                                                v (synthesizes)
                                                   [ G3P (Sugar Building Block) ]
```

- **Water photolysis**: $H_2O$ is split at PS II, yielding electrons ($e^-$) and producing **`O2`**.
- **Photosystem II**: Labeled **`PS II`**, contains **`Chlorophyll`**, driven by absorption of **`Light`**.
- **Intermediate complex**: **`Cytochrome Complex`** receives electrons and pumps **`Protons (H+)`**.
- **ATP Generation**: **`ATP Synthase`** uses the proton gradient to synthesize **`ATP`**.
- **Photosystem I**: Contains **`Chlorophyll`**, absorbs **`Light`**, transfers **`Electrons`** to reduce $NADP^+$ to **`NADPH`**.
- **Light-Independent Reactions**: **`Calvin Cycle`** in the stroma utilizes **`ATP`**, **`NADPH`**, and atmospheric **`CO2`** to synthesize **`G3P (Sugar Building Block)`**.

### Slide 9: Herbicides Inhibition Analysis
*(Corresponds to classroom board photo: [`PXL_20261012_153822713.RAW-01.jpg`](file:///home/owen/ucf/so/s1/BSC/block3/active_learning/deck_answers/PXL_20261012_153822713.RAW-01.jpg))*

#### Observation Table:
| Herbicide | Chlorophyll Absorption | Oxygen Production | NADPH Production | ATP Synthase Activity | G3P Production | Target Step / Component |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **H-1** | Normal | Normal | **Decreased** | Normal | **Decreased** | **Photosystem I** |
| **H-2** | **Decreased** | **Decreased** | **Decreased** | **Decreased** | **Decreased** | **Chlorophyll Absorption** |
| **H-3** | Normal | **Decreased** | **Decreased** | **Decreased** | **Decreased** | **Photosystem II** |
| **H-4** | Normal | Normal | Normal | Normal | **Decreased** | **Calvin Cycle** |
| **H-5** | Normal | Normal | Normal | **Decreased** | **Decreased** | **ATP Synthase** |

#### Step-by-Step Diagnostic Rationale:
1. **H-1 $\rightarrow$ Photosystem I**:
   - $O_2$ production is normal (PS II splits water normally).
   - ATP Synthase is normal (proton pumping across cytochrome complex is intact).
   - Only NADPH is decreased because PS I cannot pass electrons to $NADP^+$. Without NADPH, the Calvin cycle stalls (G3P decreases).
2. **H-2 $\rightarrow$ Chlorophyll Absorption**:
   - Primary light harvesting is impaired. Since chlorophyll cannot absorb light, all downstream light-dependent and light-independent processes crash ($O_2$, NADPH, ATP, G3P all decrease).
3. **H-3 $\rightarrow$ Photosystem II**:
   - PS II is where water splitting occurs; blocking PS II immediately halts $O_2$ release.
   - Without electrons initiated at PS II, linear electron flow to the cytochrome complex and PS I cannot proceed, crashing ATP, NADPH, and G3P.
4. **H-4 $\rightarrow$ Calvin Cycle**:
   - All thylakoid light reactions (light absorption, $O_2$ release, proton pumping, ATP synthesis, NADPH production) run normally.
   - Only G3P synthesis is suppressed, indicating a direct lesion in the Calvin cycle enzyme machinery (e.g., Rubisco).
5. **H-5 $\rightarrow$ ATP Synthase**:
   - Light absorption, $O_2$ release, and linear electron flow producing NADPH remain normal.
   - ATP synthase activity is specifically blocked. Without ATP, the endergonic reduction and regeneration phases of the Calvin cycle stall, lowering G3P yield.
